Since supply chain security leaders are all chiming in on the usefulness of <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/signup/cold-join?session_redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.linkedin.com%2Ffeed%2Fhashtag%2Fsboms&amp;trk=public_post-text" class="link" target="_self" data-tracking-control-name="public_post-text" data-tracking-will-navigate="">#SBOMs</a>, I'd like to share some actual <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/signup/cold-join?session_redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.linkedin.com%2Ffeed%2Fhashtag%2Fdata&amp;trk=public_post-text" class="link" target="_self" data-tracking-control-name="public_post-text" data-tracking-will-navigate="">#data</a> and <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/signup/cold-join?session_redirect=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.linkedin.com%2Ffeed%2Fhashtag%2Fanalysis&amp;trk=public_post-text" class="link" target="_self" data-tracking-control-name="public_post-text" data-tracking-will-navigate="">#analysis</a> done by our friends at <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/school/new-york-university/?trk=public_post-text" class="link" target="_self" data-tracking-control-name="public_post-text" data-tracking-will-navigate="">New York University</a> 👇 SBOMs are not very useful. In fact, in an analysis done by NYU, they would only prevent about 10% of historical supply chain attacks. However, when SBOM is combined with in-toto, they found that 87% of supply chain attacks would have been prevented. Here’s how different approaches stack up: - Basic SBOM: 10% protection against historical attacks. - Signed SBOM: Slight improvement, covering 35% of historical attacks. - SLSA / in-toto simple case: 51% protection, though locating in-toto metadata can be challenging. - in-toto + SBOM (SBOMit project Phase 1): Builds in-toto directly into the SBOM, covering 51% and making it easier to locate relevant metadata while helping to prevent SBOM manipulation. - SBOM generated from in-toto metadata (SBOMit project Phase 2): Extends Phase 1 with more robust protections, reaching 87% effectiveness and deriving accurate data from in-toto metadata. Are SBOMs a silver bullet? No, but when combined with other technology, they can significantly reduce the attack surface of the software supply chain. Links to the raw data and SBOMit project are in the comments cc <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/iandh?trk=public_post-text" class="link" target="_self" data-tracking-control-name="public_post-text" data-tracking-will-navigate="">Ian Dunbar-Hall</a>, <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/yuchen-dennis-zhang-5bb98813a?trk=public_post-text" class="link" target="_self" data-tracking-control-name="public_post-text" data-tracking-will-navigate="">Yuchen (Dennis) Zhang</a>
